I didn't have many annotations on this aptly named paper as I read it, but I thought about it many times after. Some thoughts:
- The paper seems to be arguing for generalist models, and an Occam's razor approach to pursuing AGI. Insofar as this argument is a proponent of LLMs over specific ML techniques, I follow. However, it makes two statements that feel conflicting: "building in how we think we think does not work in the long run," and, "We want AI agents that can discover like we can, not which contain what we have discovered".
    - I'm not sure what this second statement means in practice. No clear analogies come to my mind - educators, parents, and other existing computational models all encode knowledge during the process of teaching "search and learning".
- When thinking about the theory of what the paper proposes, I see benefits:
    - Observing models that think like we think, to see if they come to similar conclusions, will teach us a lot about humans as they exist today. In particular, I wonder if these models will be able to avoid bias, find mistakes in discoveries we consider axiomatic, etc.
-  I would like to think more about what this means for interpretability, which I consider a fundamental value.
