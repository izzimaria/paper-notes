Note: I'm summarizing this reflection a few years later, so I'm sure some of these comments will suffer from hindsight bias.

A few thoughts that rise to the surface:
- AI will be bound by some of the currently accepted physical limits of the world. However, there will be others whose boundaries AI pushes, that end up being axioms that are disproven. It will be interesting to follow these.
- Apart from the 10%-90% argument, there may also be roles where human labor is cheaper and requires less overhead than what AI models would cost, particularly in the physical world.
- Solving biological problems seems to be the primary incentive for pursuing AGI (at least, it is marketed that way, likely because it resonates). I think until we see a somewhat major breakthrough here, it will be hard for the general public to feel like they personally benefit from AI progress. The negative economic and cognitive effects are felt far sooner.
    - Some of these, such as anti-aging/cryopreservation, will be controversial.
- I think diffusion will be the bottleneck here - SF moves faster than the rest of the country in terms of early adoption. I'm not sure that the entity (company, country, etc) with the best models will always win if its not in everyone's hands.
- It seems that the appropriate analogue societal impacts wise is social media, and economic impacts wise is the industrial revolution.
- Life post-AGI seems to be marketed as a time where we can do what we enjoy all the time, but I think scarcity can be, to some extent, necessary.
- I will be curious to see if, as time goes on, models inform people when they are unsure of the answer, or continue to hallucinate. As far as agents go, hallucination is considered dishonesty, and will affect adoption.
